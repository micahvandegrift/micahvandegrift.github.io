# No Wave and Sonic Youth

*Topic 2 of the course archive.*

## Overview

This topic starts in New York with No Wave, an offshoot of late-1970s punk that rejected melody and technique, and then follows Sonic Youth, a band that came out of punk, mixed it with No Wave and tied it to experimental music. Formed in New York in 1981, they went from dissonant tunings and "prepared" instruments to a major label deal, and in 2007 one of their albums was added to the Library of Congress.

Sonic Youth are a case study in what the underground could do. They asked to be taken seriously as art, reached back before punk for influence, treated relationships and networking as currency, and brought the idea of "negation" back into rock. In the words of the reading, they were "more an inspiration than an influence."

## Key ideas

**No Wave**

* **An offshoot of late-1970s punk,** mainly in New York, 1978 to 1982. The name plays off the New Wave bands of the day: The B-52's, The Cars, Devo, Talking Heads.
* **The look:** choppy haircuts and skinny suits and ties.
* **Different from punk.** No Wave rejected melody as well as technique. It put musical texture ahead of tone or style. It was atonal and dissonant.
* **As much performance art as music.** It was iconoclastic: all about tearing down the foundations of music.
* ***No New York* (1978).** Brian Eno put this compilation together to document the scene. He first considered ten bands but ended up with four, four songs each: The Contortions, Teenage Jesus and the Jerks, Mars and DNA.

**Sonic Youth's sound**

* Influences they named: Glenn Branca, The Stooges, The Velvet Underground, Black Sabbath, MC5 and the Grateful Dead.
* They were deeply affected by seeing Minor Threat perform in May 1982 (Azerrad, p. 238).
* They got their sound from a variety of tunings, and from "prepared" instruments, much like the experiments of Henry Cowell and John Cage. They specialized in dissonant tones. (The notes call for a music theory review of consonant versus dissonant.)

**What they brought to the underground** (from Azerrad)

* **Timbre, texture and rhythm.** They put a premium on timbre (the character or quality of a musical sound as distinct from its pitch and intensity), harmonic texture and rhythm.
* **Art.** They asked to be taken seriously as art.
* **Nostalgia.** They allowed their music to be nostalgic, reaching back before punk for influence.
* **Relationships as currency.** Empowerment came through grassroots connections, as opposed to the mainstream, where bands are marketed into existence.
* **A framework for indie rock.** They carried the music from punk's working-class roots to urban high art, and pioneered the search for new sounds in rock (p. 243).
* **Negation.** They brought the concept of "negation" back into rock (p. 249): the refusal to ignore harsh realities; the world is not as it seems. Greil Marcus coined the term in a 1983 *Artforum* piece on Sonic Youth. It was used by 1960s bands such as CCR, MC5, The Stooges and The Velvet Underground, and was a reaction against Tom Petty, John Mellencamp and Bruce Springsteen.
* **Networking.** They recognized the importance of networking and put it into practice, carrying many other bands to notoriety alongside them (pp. 254-256).
* **Physical versus cerebral.** This is the line drawn between hardcore and pre-indie, for instance Minor Threat compared with The Flaming Lips. It plays into underground music for the rest of its life.
* **High art and low art.** Like Warhol, they mixed pop culture with high art. Arty versus edgy (p. 264).
* **"More an inspiration than an influence"** (p. 273).

## Who's who

**No Wave**

* The Contortions, Teenage Jesus and the Jerks, Mars and DNA: the four bands on *No New York*.
* Brian Eno, who produced the *No New York* compilation.

**Sonic Youth**

* Members: Thurston Moore, Kim Gordon, Lee Ranaldo, Steve Shelley.
* Former members: Bob Bert, Richard Edson (the first drummer), Jim O'Rourke, Jim Sclavunos.

**People along the way**

* The Swans, who toured with them in November 1982 (the "Savage Blunder Tour").
* Ian MacKaye, interviewed in the first issue of Moore's fanzine, *Killer*.
* Bruce Pavitt, who met them in Seattle in January 1985 and paid $100 to use "Kill Yr. Idols" on a compilation called *Sub Pop 100*.
* Greg Ginn of Black Flag, whose SST Records they signed to in 1986.
* Chuck D, guest on "Kool Thing."
* Spike Jonze and Jason Lee, in the video for "100%."

## Sonic Youth timeline

* **1981:** Formed in New York City. In December they record an EP for Glenn Branca's Neutral Records.
* **November 1982:** Tour with the Swans.
* **1983:** Jim Sclavunos joins on drums and they release *Confusion Is Sex*. A two-week European tour that summer is well received. Moore begins publishing the fanzine *Killer*.
* **1984:** A disastrous show in London results in good press in the English magazines *Sounds* and *NME*. *Bad Moon Rising* comments on American culture at the time.
* **1985:** The video for "Death Valley '69," their first, is filmed. It shows most of the band in various states of bloody dismemberment, mixed with live footage, and features both the recently departed Bob Bert and the new drummer, Steve Shelley.
* **Early 1986:** They sign to SST Records and record *EVOL* in March. The album is obsessed with celebrity and gets good press in the U.S., in *People* and the *New York Times*. It is an artistic breakthrough for the band (Azerrad, p. 263).
* **1987:** *Sister*, partly inspired by the life and works of Philip K. Dick. It sold 60,000 copies and cracked the *Village Voice* Pazz & Jop top 20.
* **1988:** The double LP *Daydream Nation*, released on Enigma and distributed by Capitol, partly owned by EMI. CMJ, *Rolling Stone* and others laud it as one of the best albums of the year. Distribution problems arise, and the band looks for a major label deal. The opening track is "Teen Age Riot."
* **1990:** *Goo* on Geffen Records, with the single "Kool Thing" featuring Chuck D. It is considered a more accessible album.
* **1991:** *The Year Punk Broke*, a documentary of their tour that year with Nirvana.
* **1992:** *Dirty* on DGC. The video for "100%" is by the newly discovered Spike Jonze and features Jason Lee skateboarding.
* **1994:** *Experimental Jet Set* on DGC, with "Bull in the Heather" as a pseudo-hit. No full tour, because Kim Gordon was pregnant.
* **1995:** They headline Lollapalooza.
* **1996:** They appear on *The Simpsons*, in the episode "Homerpalooza."
* **Late 1990s:** They begin releasing albums on their own label, SYR Records, based in Hoboken, NJ. *SYR3* is the first to feature Jim O'Rourke as a full member. *SYR4* (1999) is made up of covers of avant-garde composers: John Cage, Steve Reich and Yoko Ono.
* **2000 to 2006:** *NYC Ghosts and Flowers* (2000), *Murray Street* (2002), *Sonic Nurse* (2004), *Rather Ripped* (2006).
* **May 24, 2007:** *Daydream Nation* is added to the Library of Congress.
