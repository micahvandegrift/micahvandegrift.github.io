# New Weird America and the Folk Re-revival

## Overview

Moving from the 1990s into the 2000s, there is again a shift in the underground. It is probably due to the popularization of certain elements (emo, indie) and to technological advances in music production and communication. This lesson follows one thread of that shift: the folk re-revival. Elliott Smith is the precursor, a songwriter who took steps toward a folk revival, using songs as stories and approaching music as art. After him comes "New Weird America," the psychedelic folk movement of the early 2000s, which makes direct references and homage to the 1960s.

## Elliott Smith

Elliott Smith died in 2003. He was proficient at many instruments and began his solo career in 1994, and became famous through movies, including *Good Will Hunting* and the films of Wes Anderson. He dealt with depression for many years. The themes of his songs came mainly from philosophy and literature. Musically he was heavily influenced by The Beatles, Bob Dylan and Neil Young, as well as many contemporary artists.

His first album, *Roman Candle*, was on Cavity Search, his next two were on Kill Rock Stars, and then he moved to DreamWorks. He said of his music: "I like folk songs, but it is a very defined genre, and I think it's not really what I play. For me, the difference between folk and pop is that in folk, there is a clear message in every song and there is usually a moral to the story. That's fine, but it's not how I write. I like more 'impressionistic' things, word pastings. Pop is broader, more things can be in it together." And: "At first, I thought of it as storytelling. It's never seemed confessional to me. I don't need people to understand what it is to be me. It's more like dreams… Pieces are me and pieces are other people and pieces are some character I'm making up."

His influence comes from the steps he took toward a folk revival, using songs as stories and approaching his music as art, and as inspired by art. The direct references and homage to the 1960s are also an important connection, one that gets played up in New Weird America.

## New Weird America

"New Weird America" is the term used to describe the psychedelic folk movement of the early 2000s. It is generally believed to have been coined by David Keenan in the August 2003 issue of *The Wire*, after the Brattleboro Free Folk Festival. It plays on "Old Weird America," a term used by Greil Marcus for the lineage connecting early American folk performers to 1960s folk revivalists like Bob Dylan.

Musically it pulls from the psychedelic folk of the 1960s and 1970s as well as heavy metal, free jazz, electronic music, early American folk and American experimental composers. *Arthur* magazine covered it extensively, put on a festival (ArthurFest, Los Angeles, September 2005), and released a compilation called *The Golden Apples of the Sun* in 2004. It was not a unified movement, and many of the artists do not associate themselves with the label. Of "freak folk," Banhart told the *New York Times* in 2004, "it's cool, you have to call it something, but we didn't name it. We've been thinking about what to call it, and we just call it the Family." Ed Droste of Grizzly Bear has said, "Freak-folk comes with an image attached: you have to have a beard and be Jesusy, if you know what I mean. You have to have a really extreme voice, and be really divisive."

The artists associated with New Weird America and freak folk include Akron/Family, Devendra Banhart, Danielson Famile, Espers, Jack Rose, Joanna Newsom, Sufjan Stevens, M. Ward, Iron and Wine, Bonnie "Prince" Billy, Little Wings, Smog, and Wooden Wand and the Vanishing Voice. The music ranges from uber-indie to semi-popular. Many people may have heard of Sufjan Stevens or Iron and Wine, but many more may never hear of Ariel Pink. It goes from weird to aesthetically pleasing.

### Scenes and collectives

The major scene is Northern California (the Bay Area, San Francisco and Oakland), the same area where the Beats in the 1950s and the hippies in the 1960s congregated. Bands there include the Skygreen Leopards, Gris Gris and Thee Oh Sees. The music is often associated with new folk collectives: groups of people who play in one another's bands, multi-instrumentalists.

* **Elephant 6** began in Ruston, Louisiana, in the late 1980s, and became centered in Athens, Georgia, and Denver, Colorado. Associated bands include The Apples in Stereo, Neutral Milk Hotel and of Montreal.
* **Wooden Wand and the Vanishing Voice** is a collaboration between James Jackson Toth and Jessica Toth (Wooden Wand) and The Vanishing Voice, a Brooklyn band made up of G. Lucas Crane, Heidi Diehl and Steven Jarvis Tavaniere, who met at Purchase College.
* **Animal Collective** is based in New York City, though the members are from Baltimore. They are experimental musicians who met in school and recorded together and separately: Avey Tare (David Portner), Panda Bear (Noah Lennox), Geologist (Brian Weitz) and Deakin (Josh Dibb). They founded Paw Tracks Records.
* **The Skygreen Leopards** began in 2001 as a duo, Glenn Donaldson and Donovan Quinn, in San Francisco; by 2003 they were performing as a band, and have also performed as the Giant Skyflower Band, mixing members from different bands.
* **Ariel Pink** is an avant-garde "hauntology" musician from Los Angeles. He self-produces, began recording on an eight-track Portastudio, and has a lo-fi sound. He has been making tapes since 1996, and got attention when he gave a CD-R of *Worn Copy* to Animal Collective in 2003, who signed him to their label, Paw Tracks.

### Hauntology and radical traditionalism

Hauntology, as used about this music, refers to work that looks back at a half-remembered past: toward the "ghost" of earlier eras, including ones that now seem rustic, bizarre or old-timey. Jim Jupp, who co-founded Ghost Box in 2004 with the graphic designer Julian House, has described the label's work as using the past to create an imaginary world rather than as nostalgia.

The composer Ralph Shapey was called a "radical traditionalist" by critics, and the label pleased him: his music pairs a modernist, angular language with romantic concerns.
