# Metal

## Overview

Between 1983 and 1984, by one widely repeated figure, heavy metal went from about an eight percent to about a twenty percent share of all recordings sold in the U.S. Magazines devoted to the genre were launched, including *Kerrang!* in 1981 and *Metal Hammer* in 1984, along with a host of fan journals. In 1985, *Billboard* declared that the metal audience was no longer the exclusive domain of male teenagers: it had become older, younger and more female.

So if metal is so popular, how does it fit our definition of "underground"? Metal was one of the first underground musics to span the mainstream and the underground at the same time. This lesson looks at the underground end of the genre, extreme metal and its four subgenres (thrash, death, black and doom), and then at the Melvins, a band that represents underground metal differently from the extreme bands: for them it is a chance to be weird, and sometimes just plain bizarre, for the sake of enjoying the music.

## Why study extreme metal?

The sociologist Keith Kahn-Harris, author of *Extreme Metal: Music and Culture on the Edge*, gives reasons to study it, three of which are listed here.

* **It is influential.** "Extreme Metal has provided a crucial if sometimes unacknowledged motor of innovation within metal. It is increasingly becoming a source of inspiration for cutting edge artists in a number of musical fields."
* **It is artistically challenging.** "Extreme metal challenges notions of what music really is. These musicians have pioneered sounds that can be heard nowhere else…"
* **It is globally and culturally diverse.**

## The four subgenres

Metal as a whole is an extremely wide genre: fast to slow, loud to soft, underground to mainstream. It is a worldwide movement. The key elements that make it fit into the underground are the sense of community that comes with being a metalhead, the fashion and aesthetic, and the music. Four subgenres: thrash, death, black and doom.

### Thrash

Thrash is fast. Its guitar riffs sit low, are complex and tightly controlled, and are often palm muted, while the high-register solos are played at speed.

Metallica's *Kill 'Em All* (1983) is widely regarded as the first thrash metal album, and songs such as Metallica's "Hit the Lights" (written in 1981) and Accept's "Fast as a Shark" (1982) are often cited among the first thrash songs. The "big four" are Anthrax, Megadeth, Metallica and Slayer. In 1984: Anthrax's *Fistful of Metal*, Metallica's *Ride the Lightning* and Slayer's *Haunting the Chapel* EP. In 1985: Megadeth's *Killing Is My Business… and Business Is Good!* The major thrash scenes were the Bay Area, with Testament and Metallica, and New York and New Jersey, with Anthrax and Overkill.

### Death metal

Death metal usually has fast, heavily distorted guitars tuned low and played with palm muting and tremolo picking. The drumming is quick and aggressive, with blast beats and double bass, and the vocals are deep, guttural growls. The golden years of the genre were 1988 to 1994.

The term's origin is disputed. It appears around 1983, when it was often used for what we now call thrash, and it was picked up for lyrical content, for a fanzine and for a split album called *Death Metal*, and for Possessed's 1984 demo of that name, whose singer, Jeff Becerra, says he coined it. A Florida journalist is also said to have spoken of the band Death as "Death's metal." Bands include Deicide, Morbid Angel, Death and Obituary. Seminal early recordings: Death's demo *Death by Metal* (1984) and Possessed's demo *Death Metal* (1984), followed by Possessed's debut album *Seven Churches* (1985).

**Florida death metal.** Florida has its own brand, concentrated in Tampa, with bands like Assuck, Morbid Angel and Deicide. It is more direct and brutal than other death subgenres. As Natalie Purcell, a sociologist at UC Santa Cruz, writes in *Death Metal Music: The Passion and Politics of a Subculture*: "Often guitars are not downtuned as low, and the guitar work is more tight, precise and clear. Extremely fast, machine gun like blast beats characterize the drumming in Floridian Death Metal." Morrisound Studios in Tampa is where many of these records were made.

**Grindcore.** Grindcore is a close cousin of death metal. It developed in the UK in the mid-1980s with bands like Napalm Death. The name is said to have been introduced by Napalm Death's drummer Mick Harris, who says: "Grindcore came from 'grind', which was the only word I could use to describe Swans after buying their first record in '84" (quoted in Albert Mudrian, *Choosing Death*, p. 35). It is seen as a progression of hardcore punk: fast, short, loud songs, and characterized by microsongs. In 2001 Guinness World Records recognized Brutal Truth's video for "Collateral Damage," from their 1994 album *Extreme Conditions Demand Extreme Responses*, as the shortest music video (a few seconds long; sources give four seconds and 2.18 seconds). Napalm Death's "You Suffer," from 1987, at about 1.3 seconds, was listed as the shortest song.

Assuck came out of this world, as did power violence, and then emo power violence, like Reversal of Man.

### Doom

Doom is directly related to Black Sabbath and Pentagram. It is very slow, very low and very riff-based, and meant to be very loud. It is the most underground form of metal, barely recognized by metal fans in the 1980s. Trouble was among the first doom bands to bring Christian imagery and themes into its lyrics, which led them to be seen as "weird" and unacceptable by some metalheads. Many later doom bands were not Christian, but Candlemass and Saint Vitus also used religious imagery, as a symbol of pain and suffering and not as a statement of belief.

Doom spawned two offshoots:

* **Stoner doom.** Sleep's *Holy Mountain* (1992). Their third album, recorded in 1994 and known as *Dopesmoker*, was one song more than an hour long, and the record label, London Records, would not put it out as it was. A 52-minute edit called *Jerusalem* was released without the band's authorization in 1999, and *Dopesmoker* itself came out in 2003. After the breakup, the drummer and bassist went on to form Om, whose music is similar to Tibetan chant.
* **Drone doom.** Noise and ambient music, typically with songs ten to thirty minutes long, sometimes without drums, with an emphasis on volume, texture and physically embracing the music. The major bands are Sunn O))) and Earth. Earth formed in Olympia in 1989 (Dylan Carlson, Slim Moon, Greg Babior), and their album *Hex* (2005) is said to have been influenced by Neil Young's soundtrack to *Dead Man*. Sunn O))) was formed in Seattle in 1998 by Stephen O'Malley and Greg Anderson.

There is also a New Wave of American Metal: Baroness, Isis, Pelican and Torche, on the labels Hydra Head and Southern Lord.

## The Melvins

The Melvins are sludge metal, pre-grunge metal, doom, stoner, all of the above. Buzz Osborne is the singer and guitarist and Dale Crover the drummer, with various bassists and second guitarists. Their original bassist, Matt Lukin, went on to Mudhoney.

They formed in 1983 in Montesano, Washington, rehearsing in Aberdeen once Dale Crover joined, and moved to San Francisco in 1988. The name was taken from a supervisor at the grocery store where Buzz worked as a clerk. They are often compared to Black Sabbath, though Osborne has said that his guitar playing is more influenced by Black Flag.

In 1986 they released *Six Songs* on C/Z Records, and in 1987 Alchemy Records released their first full album, *Gluey Porch Treatments*. In May 1989 they recorded and released *Ozma*, with Lori Black ("Lorax"), Shirley Temple's daughter, on bass. In August 1990 Nirvana was without a drummer, and Crover filled in on a seven-date West Coast tour with Sonic Youth. Osborne introduced Dave Grohl, of Scream, to the band, and Grohl joined in September 1990 and became Nirvana's final full-time drummer. *Bullhead* followed in 1991 on Boner Records ("Ligature" is on it). In 1992 they released "solo" albums by King Buzzo, Dale Crover and Joe Preston, modeled on the solo records KISS released in 1978.

Because of Nirvana's fame, the Melvins were picked up by Atlantic in 1993 and released *Houdini* ("Joan of Arc" is on it). Atlantic dropped them in 1997 after three albums (*Houdini*, *Stoner Witch* and *Stag*). They have worked with Mike Patton's Ipecac Recordings since 1999, which also releases Hella, Isis and The Locust. They have also released albums under different band names, and collaborated with Jello Biafra on a few, as "Jelvins."

In a 1988 interview with *Maximum Rocknroll*, Buzz says, "We sound like Black Sabbath," and Dale says, "We sound like twisted Sabbath." About his lyrics, Buzz says, "We're not set out to be political… the vocals are inspired by the music, I think the lyrics are just as important as the music. They're not a social commentary or anything." And: "We are serious about our music, we are serious about this band, just in normal everyday life we like to have a lot of fun."

There is a lot of mystery around this band. They never really tell the truth and seem to have a lot of fun confusing the interviewer and the audience. Buzz has appeared in videos by Beck, the Offspring, Nine Inch Nails and Primus. Frank Kozik has often done their cover art.

**The point of the Melvins:** they are a good example of how metal spans the mainstream and the underground. They are a notable doom band who influenced bands like Mastodon, Tool and Boris.
