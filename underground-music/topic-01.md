# Introduction, Roots and Historical Context

*Topic 1 of the course archive.*

## Overview

Why have a course on underground music? This first topic sets up the big questions, then goes looking for the music's roots. For our purposes, underground music was born out of two distinctly American streams of music: experimental classical music and punk. It has a dual nature, both intellectual and aggressive, and it grew out of a long American habit of being experimental and progressive.

The story of this music works in and out of chronological order. There are brief periods where you can see a movement, and then it disappears. We look at it over time, and now and again pause to look at the culture that grew up alongside the music. The important thing to remember is that this music is about progression, pushing the envelope and stretching the ear.

The second half of the topic is context. Art is always a product of its time, so to understand it we start with the age it came from: the 1980s, which are known for two things, politics and entertainment. Underneath all of it came a reaction, an underground that was aesthetically, philosophically and politically opposed to the mainstream.

## Key ideas

* **Two American streams.** Experimental classical music and punk. America became the torch-bearer of new cultural movements over the twentieth century, and the rugged individualism and think-for-yourself attitude of its people spurred that on.
* **"What is music?"** John Cage was one of the first to ask. His *4'33"* consisted of whatever unintentional and ambient sounds the audience heard during the framed time. Indeterminacy, he said, is an invitation not to anarchy but to freedom.
* **Noise versus "so-called musical sounds."** In the past the point of disagreement was between dissonance and consonance. Cage predicted it would be between noise and so-called musical sounds (Gann, p. 130).
* **Minimalism seeps in.** The audacity of the American experimentalists to question centuries of musical tradition set the world on a course that eventually led to punk and post-punk being accepted as some form of art.
* **Experimental ideas go into rock.** The Velvet Underground took what they learned from experimental composers and began to use it in rock. As Brian Eno put it, only 1,000 people bought the Velvet record, but almost all of them were inspired to start a band.
* **Punk is short, fast, loud and angry.** Its brazen attitude and its bold, know-nothing, just-do-it mentality feed into the underground, especially in the early 1980s.
* **Three important bands.** The Velvet Underground, The Stooges and Black Sabbath.
* **The pop and underground split.** In the early 1980s, Big Media was so focused on pushing the top level of pop culture that underneath it an entirely different culture developed, built from the ground up by disenchanted youth.
* **Negation.** The commercials of the 1980s show a glitzy, glamorous world. That is not how it was, and much of underground culture is based on the idea of negation: things are not the way they seem.
* **Where the epicenters were.** The centers of the underground sat in the same regions that produced the political and popular culture of the decade: Washington, DC (politics) and Los Angeles (Hollywood).
* **Room for new ideas.** The split between pop culture and the underground, and the youth's utter rejection of "the norm," set up the space where DIY, indie, and the question of what the underground includes or excludes came into existence.

## Who's who

**The experimentalists**

* John Cage (1912-1992). First performance of *4'33"* on August 29, 1952. Inspired by his friend Robert Rauschenberg's white paintings.
* Philip Glass and La Monte Young (minimalism). Along with Cage, part of the New York School, a name that later became synonymous with avant-garde and minimalism. The notes also list Glass as an inspiration to Bowie, Harry Partch, Milton Babbitt, Brian Eno and John Zorn.
* Glenn Branca. In the early 1980s he began composing for guitar orchestras, or as we call them, "guitarchestras."

**From experiment to rock**

* John Cale and Lou Reed, The Velvet Underground (1965). Cale came to America to study and play with La Monte Young. The Velvets were the foundation for 1960s garage rock.

**Punk**

* The Stooges (Ann Arbor, MI). Their 1969 debut was produced by John Cale. Three studio albums, then burned out on drugs.
* MC5 (Motor City 5, Detroit, MI).
* New York punk: The Ramones, Patti Smith, and Richard Hell and The Voidoids.
* Richard Hell played with Television and released "Blank Generation" in 1977. It is said that he created the look of punk, and that Malcolm McLaren modeled the Sex Pistols on him.

## The 1980s at a glance

**Politics**

* High inflation and unemployment, "the Misery Index," led many to vote for Reagan, who was popular with many groups despite his policies.
* The New Conservatism came out of the New Right, or the New Christian Right, which grew out of the evangelical movements of the 1970s. It called for a return to fundamental values. Who decides those fundamental values?
* Reaganomics: victory over inflation, deregulation of industry, a moratorium on social spending, enormous tax cuts and a staggering budget deficit. Great wealth to some, but the percentage of Americans in poverty increased.
* The birth of the Yuppies (Young Urban Professionals): mostly white, well educated, living in urban condos, on fast-track careers, with leisure time spent on a lavish lifestyle. They set consumption patterns many people tried to copy.
* Rights for women and gays had another rise. AIDS was identified in 1981.

**Music and media**

* The music business was in a slump, riding on the shoulders of a few: Michael Jackson, Whitney Houston, Madonna, Prince, Bruce Springsteen, Phil Collins, Janet Jackson.
* New competition: videos, video games, cable, and the "pirating" of music by copying tapes.
* MTV launched August 1, 1981, with "Video Killed the Radio Star," and totally changed the way music was marketed. *Thriller* in 1982 forced MTV to change its format to include Black artists.
* The production of celebrity became a huge business and began to drive the commercialization of American culture.

**Technology**

* The birth of the information age: the PC in 1977, cell phones in 1983, the space race, video games, MTV.
* The first CDs went on sale in 1983, and by 1988 they had surpassed vinyl in sales. Tapes passed vinyl for the first time, thanks to boomboxes and Walkmen.
* Globalization: the world gets smaller. Economists used the term in 1981 to describe countries coming to terms with being connected to one earth, in business, entertainment, technology and culture. The rate of change was exponentially more rapid than in previous decades.

**1984**

* U.S. involvement in civil wars in El Salvador and Nicaragua, banks failing, homelessness on the rise, and crack introduced to the streets.
* Reagan's campaign slogan: "It is morning in America!"
* Bruce Springsteen released *Born in the USA*, with songs about working-class people around 35 years old facing economic or personal difficulties and a sense of better times past.
