# Is "Underground" Still Possible?

## Overview

Moving from the 1990s into the 2000s, there is again a shift in the underground, probably due to the popularization of certain elements (emo, indie) and to technological advances in music production and communication. What is the definition of indie now? Does it reflect the history of this subculture? Is "underground" still possible?

By the late 2000s, the timeline of the underground was almost long enough to start to see patterns. In cultural movements there is often a movement followed by a counter-movement. Is there a pendulum swinging from one extreme to another in this subculture, from art to action?

## Discussion questions

* What is the definition of indie now? Does it reflect the history of this subculture?
* Is underground still possible?
* Is there a pendulum swinging from one extreme to another in our subculture? Art to action?
* Is it a fair assumption to say that the indie underground is moving more toward artistic and intellectual sensibilities, or toward aggressive ones?
* Where will the next Seattle be?

## Where things stand

By the late 2000s it could be said that there was a fan culture surrounding this music: a distinct culture, often divorced from its history, yet pulling from it. A lot of the precedents set by the early underground are being reflected in new ways.

For example, DIY was adopted into technology, and by the late 2000s it was coming out of it again, with a return to print zines, 7-inch records, LPs and house shows.

Is it a fair assumption that the indie underground is moving more toward artistic and intellectual sensibilities, or toward aggressive ones? In certain genres, yes. The folk re-revival and New Weird America (Lesson 13) are one example of the artistic direction.
