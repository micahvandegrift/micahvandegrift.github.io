# Week 1: Introduction and Historical Context

Why have a course on underground music? The first week sets up the big questions, traces the music's American roots in experimental classical music and punk, and looks at the politics and pop culture of the 1980s that the underground pushed back against.

### 1. Construct a brief history of underground music.

- There is a precedent in American Music to be experimental and progressive. (American Music in the 20<sup>th</sup> Century- Kyle Gann)
- Dual nature of this music intellectual and aggressive
- For our purposes Underground music was essentially born out of two distinctly American streams of music: Experimental Classical music and Punk.
- It is important to recognize the relationship of these ideas with America. Over the course of the 20<sup>th</sup> century America became the torch-bearer of new cultural movements, and the rugged individualism and think for yourself attitude of the American people spurred this on.
  - John Cage (1912-1992) – one of the first to question, “What is music?”
    - Aug. 29<sup>th</sup> 1952 first performance of *4’33’’* Play example
    - It consisted of whatever unintentional and ambient sounds the audience heard during the framed time period.
    - “I believe that the use of noise to make music will continue and increase until we reach a music produced through the aid of electrical instruments which will make available for musical purposes any and all sounds that can be heard… whereas, in the past, the point of disagreement has been between dissonance and consonance, it will be, in the immediate future, between noise and so-called musical sounds.” Gann , 130
    - indeterminacy is an invitation not to anarchy but to freedom.
    - Music is continuous, it is we who turn away – from India
    - The idea of minimalism begins to seep into art during this time period. The audacity of the American experimentalists to question centuries of musical tradition set the world on a course that eventually led to music like punk and post-punk being acceptable as some form of art. This is a trend that can be traced through the entirety of the 20<sup>th</sup> century.
    - Inspired by his friend Robert Rauschenberg- white paintings. <span class="old-link">[old link: http://www.tate.org.uk/tateetc/issue8/images/erasuregenteel_eraseddekooning.jpg]</span>

### 2. 1980’s: Music, Society and Culture

- The 80s are known for two things- politics and entertainment.
- Art is always a product of its time, so to accurately understand it your must start with the context of the age.
- What is the socio-cultural context? What was happening in the world?
- **Politics:** The country was faced with high inflation and unemployment “the Misery Index”. Led many to vote for Reagan. Popular with many groups despite his policies.
- New Conservatism- came from the New Right, or the New Christian Right. Grew out of the evangelical movements that were big in the 70s. Called for a return to fundamental values in America. Who decides those fundamental values?
- Reaganomics
  - Victory over inflation, deregulation of industry, a moratorium on social spending, enormous tax cuts, and a staggering budget deficit. Great wealth to some, but % of Americans in poverty increased.
- Birth of the Yuppies- Young Urban Professionals, mostly white, well educated, live in urban condos, fast track careers, leisure time spent on lavish lifestyle. Established consumption patterns that many people tried to emulate. American Psycho
- Rights for women and gays had another rise. AIDS identified in 1981
- **In music,** Business was in a slump, and it began to ride on the shoulders of a few: Michael Jackson, Whitney Houston, Madonna, Prince, Bruce Springsteen, Phil Collins, Janet Jackson.
- Competition in other forms of entertainment: videos, video games, cable. Also ‘pirating’ from copying tapes with cassette recorders.
- Birth of the information age: PC 1977, cell phones 1983, the space race, video games, MTV.
- MTV- 1981 Aug 1<sup>st</sup>- Video Killed the Radio Star
  - Totally changed the way music was marketed.
  - Thriller- 1982 forced MTV to change its format to include Black artists.
- The production of Celebrity became a huge business and one that began to drive the commercialization of American culture.
- Move toward globalization, the world gets smaller. Term used by economists in 1981 to describe how the countries in the world were beginning to come to terms with being connected to one earth. Business, entertainment, technology, culture, and more.
- 1983 first CDs on sale, 1988 they surpass vinyl in sales. The rate of change (technologically, trends/fads) was exponentially more rapid than in previous decades.

- It is interesting to note that the epicenters of the underground were concentrated around the same regional areas that were producing the political and popular culture of the 80s. The 2 most important Washington DC, the political capital of our nation, and L.A. (SoCal) Hollywood.
- While all this was happening, there was a reaction underneath it all that came in the form of Underground Music. It was aesthetically opposed, philosophically opposed and politically opposed to the mainstream. Was it a counterculture in the beginning that became a subculture?
- The early 80s represent, for our purposes, the point in American culture where Big Media was so concentrated on pushing the top level of pop culture that underneath it an entirely different culture developed. This underground forms the framework for the disenchanted youth and was built from the ground up by them.
- The split between pop culture and the underground in the 80s, and the utter rejection of “the norm” by these youths set up the intellectual or philosophical space where ideas like DIY, Indie and how to define what was included or excluded in the underground came into existence.
