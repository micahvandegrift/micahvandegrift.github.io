## What changed, and what appears only once

Things the syllabi mention that the 16-topic draft doesn't capture well:

- **Early indie: Guided By Voices and Pavement.** Spring 2008 and Summer 2008
  only. Fits topic 9.
- **Beat Happening, Rites of Spring, Husker Du** appear in every semester under
  the transition to the 1990s (topic 9).
- **Zine culture** is its own session in Spring 2008 and Summer 2008. Fits
  topic 6, or topic 5.
- ***The Shield Around the K* (DVD).** Summer 2008 only, with politics.
- **Florida's underground scenes.** A "Florida Underground Scene symposium"
  in Spring 2008, and a "Special Lecture: Florida's Underground Music Scenes"
  in Fall 2008 and Spring 2009. It is a possible topic of its own, or a sidebar
  on topic 12. The FSView article also mentions an April 2009 symposium on
  artistry and business in independent music.
- **Fall 2008, week 4:** day 1 was a meeting in the Strozier Library lobby and
  day 2 a **guest speaker**. The syllabus doesn't say who.
- **"B.N. and A.N." (Before Nirvana and After Nirvana)** is a Fall 2008 and
  Spring 2009 discussion.
- **A "1994: indie rock's foundational year" quiz** (Fall 2008, Spring 2009)
  is likely where the "1994-2001" listening list belongs.
- **Art:** Spring 2008 names Frank Kozik, Art Chantry, Winston Smith and Jem
  Cohen, plus film and photography, and ends with Scott Sinclair and Hot Water
  Music. Fall 2008 and Spring 2009 name Raymond Pettibon, Winston Smith and
  Art Chantry. Fall 2008 also has a quiz on Scott Sinclair's and Hot Water
  Music's album covers.
- **Midterm (Spring 2008 only):** the *Instrument* DVD was shown that week.

The notes have headings that match none of the four syllabi exactly (for
example "Week Three Theme: The Principles of the underground" and "Confusion
of Genres: Pop Culture vs. Underground"). As Micah said, the notes grew over
the semesters, so treat them as their own source, not as any one semester's
record.

## Readings by topic

From the Spring 2009 syllabus (the most detailed). Spring 2008 and Summer 2008
list fewer readings, mostly the Sinker and Azerrad chapters. Differences are
noted.

1. **Roots and context:** introductions to Azerrad, Sinker and Greenberg; Holly
   Kruse, "Telling the Story of Independent Music," pp. 5-13. (Spring 2008
   and Summer 2008: Azerrad and Sinker introductions only.)
2. **No Wave and Sonic Youth:** Thurston Moore in Sinker; Sonic Youth in
   Azerrad.
3. **Hardcore:** "American Hardcore" (course library).
4. **DIY:** Steve Albini and Ian MacKaye in Sinker; Fugazi in Azerrad; Ian
   MacKaye; Kruse, "Indie vs. Major," pp. 41-50 and 64-69; Stormy Shepherd;
   Albini, "The Problem with Music." Listening: NPR interview on
   diyordie.org.
5. **Scenes and labels:** Kruse pp. 14-28; Sara Cohen, "Scenes"; Sub Pop oral
   history; Operation Ivy and 924 Gilman (course library); Mordam Records in
   Sinker (Spring 2008, Summer 2008).
6. **Politics:** Dead Kennedys, Minor Threat, Operation Ivy and 924 Gilman
   (course library); Jello Biafra and Noam Chomsky in Sinker; Minor Threat in
   Azerrad.
8. **Metal:** Deena Weinstein, "Heavy Metal's Proud Pariahs" (in Greenberg);
   Keith Kahn-Harris, "The Scene and Transgression" (course library). (Spring
   2008 and Summer 2008: online interviews, reviews and fan sites.)
9. **Transition to the 1990s:** Duncan Barlow, Bob Mould and Ted Leo in Sinker;
   Beat Happening, Husker Du and the Epilogue in Azerrad; Greenwald ch. 1;
   Michael Dennis, "Youth Activism in the 1990s" (in Greenberg).
10. **Emo:** Greenwald introduction and chapters 2-5 and 14; Sunny Day Real
    Estate, Jeremy Enigk, Norman Brannon and Cometbus (course library);
    Jawbreaker in Sinker; J. Patrick Williams and Emily Lamison essays in
    Greenberg (pp. 104 and 160).
11. **New scenes and labels:** Vagrant Records (course library); Greenwald
    chapters 6 and 8. Listening: NPR interview with Mike and Tim Kinsella.
12. **Gender:** Greenwald ch. 9; Kathleen Hanna, Matt Wobensmith, The Gossip,
    Jody Bleyle and Sleater-Kinney in Sinker; a riot grrrl article (course
    library); Natalie Porter in Greenberg (p. 121).
13. **Religion:** Porcell in Sinker; Ray Cappo, Porcell, and Andrew Beaujon's
    chapter "The Baffled King" (course library); Robert Wood in Greenberg
    (p. 223). Listening: NPR interview with Andrew Beaujon.
14. **New Weird America:** Elliott Smith (course library); "more readings TBA."
15. **Art:** "An Artist's Eye and a Killer's Touch" in Sinker. (Spring 2008:
    Frank Kozik, Art Chantry, Winston Smith and Jem Cohen in Sinker.)
