# Underground Music in America: Master List of Topics

*Compiled October 24, 2026 from the course syllabi and the lecture notes.
Course content only; contact details, grading and policy sections are left out
on purpose.*

Part of the project plan in `underground-music-plan.md`. Micah taught this
course over several semesters and changed it each time. Which semester
something was taught in doesn't matter for the archive, so this is **one
combined list of every lesson, theme and topic**, in roughly the order the
course ran. Songs are in `underground-music-listening.md`.

Everything here comes from the syllabi (four were shared; that is all
Micah has). Where the notes cover a topic, that's listed too. I outlined the
notes from their headings and haven't read all 26,000 words, so "coverage"
is an estimate.

## The topics

### 1. Introduction, roots and historical context

- **Lessons:** why have a course on underground music; defining underground,
  indie and DIY; subcultural studies (discussion of readings, defining the terms
  of the discourse); historical precedents (the New York School composers,
  John Cage, Glass, La Monte Young; the Velvet Underground; Iggy and the
  Stooges; proto-punk); the 1980s: music, society and culture.
- **Readings:** introductions to Azerrad, Sinker and Greenberg; Holly Kruse,
  "Telling the Story of Independent Music," pp. 5-13.
- **Notes:** well covered, in the first block (about 3,800 words with topics 2 and
  3). This is the Week 1 prototype page.

### 2. No Wave and Sonic Youth

- **Lessons:** No Wave and New York City; Sonic Youth.
- **Readings:** Thurston Moore in Sinker; Sonic Youth in Azerrad.
- **Notes:** well covered, in the first block.

### 3. Hardcore

- **Lessons:** intro to hardcore; roots of the genres; skate culture.
- **Film:** *American Hardcore.*
- **Readings:** "American Hardcore" (course library).
- **Notes:** covered at the end of the first block.

### 4. Scenes and labels

- **Lessons:** what is a scene; regional and major American scenes of the
  1980s and the labels they spawned; Anatomy of a Scene: Berkeley, CA (924
  Gilman, Operation Ivy, Fifteen, Maximumrocknroll).
- **Readings:** Kruse pp. 14-28; Sara Cohen, "Scenes"; the Sub Pop oral
  history; Mordam Records in Sinker; Operation Ivy and 924 Gilman (course
  library).
- **Notes:** well covered (about 2,200 words).

### 5. Politics and communication

- **Lessons:** political culture of the 1980s; Dead Kennedys and Minor Threat;
  radical vs. personal politics; zine culture.
- **Film:** *The Shield Around the K.*
- **Readings:** Jello Biafra and Noam Chomsky in Sinker; Minor Threat in
  Azerrad; Dead Kennedys and Minor Threat (course library).
- **Notes:** well covered (about 2,000 words).

### 6. DIY, indie and the business of music

- **Lessons:** DIY: what it is and how it works; what is indie; Indie vs.
  Major, "the Great Debate"; Fugazi; pop culture vs. the underground.
- **Readings:** Steve Albini and Ian MacKaye in Sinker; Fugazi in Azerrad;
  Kruse, "Indie vs. Major," pp. 41-50 and 64-69; Stormy Shepherd; Albini,
  "The Problem with Music." Listening: NPR interview on diyordie.org.
- **Notes:** well covered (about 2,400 words on the principles of the underground,
  and part of a block on pop culture vs. underground).

### 7. Metal

- **Lessons:** underground metal (major genres, bands, musical influences); the
  Melvins.
- **Film:** *Metal: A Headbanger's Journey.*
- **Readings:** Deena Weinstein, "Heavy Metal's Proud Pariahs" (in Greenberg);
  Keith Kahn-Harris, "The Scene and Transgression"; online interviews,
  reviews and fan sites.
- **Notes:** covered (shares a block of about 1,900 words with topic 6).

### 8. The transition to the 1990s

- **Lessons:** new genres and a new socio-cultural context; lo-fi and emocore
  (Beat Happening, Rites of Spring, Husker Du); Before Nirvana and After Nirvana;
  the 1990s: music, society and culture; the changing nature of indie; early
  indie (Guided By Voices, Pavement); 1994 as indie rock's foundational year.
- **Film:** *Instrument.*
- **Readings:** Duncan Barlow, Bob Mould and Ted Leo in Sinker; Beat
  Happening, Husker Du and the Epilogue in Azerrad; Greenwald ch. 1; Michael
  Dennis, "Youth Activism in the 1990s" (in Greenberg).
- **Notes:** covered in two blocks (about 900 and 2,000 words). Guided By Voices and
  Pavement get two or three mentions.

### 9. Emo

- **Lessons:** what is emo; a brief history; Sunny Day Real Estate and
  Jawbreaker; technology and communication (tapes to mp3s, zines to
  magazines and blogs).
- **Readings:** Greenwald introduction and chapters 2-5 and 14; Sunny Day Real
  Estate, Jeremy Enigk, Norman Brannon and Cometbus (course library); Jawbreaker
  in Sinker; J. Patrick Williams and Emily Lamison in Greenberg (pp. 104, 160).
- **Notes:** well covered (about 2,400 words).

### 10. New scenes, labels, clubs and sounds

- **Lessons:** the mid-1990s labels (Jade Tree, Vagrant, No Idea, Deep Elm);
  Anatomy of a Scene: Chicago, through the eyes of the Kinsella brothers;
  Saddle Creek.
- **Film:** *Spend an Evening with Saddle Creek.*
- **Readings:** Vagrant Records (course library); Greenwald chapters 6 and 8.
  Listening: NPR interview with Mike and Tim Kinsella.
- **Notes:** covered (about 2,500 words, with topic 11).

### 11. Gender in the underground

- **Lessons:** riot grrrl; queercore; famous women of the underground.
- **Readings:** Greenwald ch. 9; Kathleen Hanna, Matt Wobensmith, The Gossip,
  Jody Bleyle and Sleater-Kinney in Sinker; a riot grrrl article (course
  library); Natalie Porter in Greenberg (p. 121).
- **Notes:** a section ("Gender Intro," "Famous Females") sits in the block with
  topic 10.

### 12. Religion in the underground

- **Lessons:** religious themes; Krishnacore; Christian hardcore and Christian
  underground music; Pedro the Lion.
- **Readings:** Porcell in Sinker; Ray Cappo, Porcell, and Andrew Beaujon's
  chapter "The Baffled King" (course library); Robert Wood in Greenberg (p.
  223). Listening: NPR interview with Andrew Beaujon.
- **Notes:** well covered (about 2,400 words).

### 13. New Weird America and the folk re-revival

- **Lessons:** the folk re-revival; Elliott Smith; new weird America.
- **Film:** *Danielson: A Family Movie.*
- **Readings:** Elliott Smith (course library); "more readings TBA."
- **Notes:** covered inside the last block.

### 14. Visual art and authenticity

- **Lessons:** art in the underground; album cover meaning and message; poster
  art; film and photography; Raymond Pettibon, Winston Smith, Art Chantry,
  Frank Kozik, Jem Cohen; Scott Sinclair and Hot Water Music's album covers.
- **Readings:** "An Artist's Eye and a Killer's Touch" and the Kozik, Chantry,
  Winston Smith and Jem Cohen interviews in Sinker.
- **Notes:** covered inside the last block (about 3,300 words for topics 13 to 15). Raymond
  Pettibon and Scott Sinclair don't appear in the notes at all.

### 15. Is "underground" still possible?

- **Lessons:** new meanings of indie; blogs; indie and the future.
- **Notes:** covered inside the last block.

### 16. Florida's underground music scenes

- **Lessons:** a special lecture, and in one version a symposium, on the Florida
  scenes. The FSView article also mentions an April 2009 symposium on
  artistry and business in independent music.
- **Notes:** eleven scattered mentions of "Florida"; no obvious dedicated
  section. Micah may remember what was said.

## Course-wide material

- **Texts:** Greenwald, *Nothing Feels Good*; Sinker, *We Owe You Nothing*;
  Azerrad, *Our Band Could Be Your Life*; Greenberg, *Youth Subcultures*;
  Kruse, *Site and Sound*. Not every text was used every time. Additional
  readings were shared through an online course library (Blackboard).
- **Films:** *American Hardcore*; *Metal: A Headbanger's Journey*;
  *Instrument*; *Spend an Evening with Saddle Creek*; *Danielson: A Family
  Movie*; *The Shield Around the K.*
- **Planning notes** (top of the lecture notes): themes to emphasize (sense of
  community; scene identity vs. subcultural identity; the "incestual quality"
  of scenes; it all revolves around the music; intensity and personal feeling;
  nostalgia; politics; high vs. low art; regionalism; skate culture;
  technology; zines) and bands to cover (Sonic Youth, Fugazi, Husker Du,
  Earth, Bikini Kill, Fifteen, and the Cap'n Jazz family of bands). These are
  already on the course home page.

## Left out on purpose

Assignments, grading, attendance, honor code, disability statements, office
and contact information, guest-speaker logistics, quizzes, and
paper-draft deadlines. The course home page keeps the description, objectives
and format.
